GuardBand

Privacy Notice

Version 1.3 · 27 October 2026

GuardBand is a student project. It is a phone app that works with a wrist-worn safety band: the band sends an alert when its wearer presses its button, and the app lets a guardian see where that alert came from. This page explains what personal data is involved, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

It describes what the app actually does, taken from the code. It is not a legal opinion and makes no claim that the project meets the requirements of any particular law or standard.

Who is responsible

GuardBand is operated by USC Evictees, a student team in the BS Information Technology programme at the University of Cebu–Banilad.

For anything on this page — a question, a correction, a request to delete your data — write to julianmiguelroldan@gmail.com.

What is collected, and why

When you make an account

DataWhy
Your name So the app can address you, and so a guardian is identifiable.
Your email address It is how you sign in. The app also sends a link to it to confirm the address is yours, and a password reset reaches you there.
Your password To sign you in. It is handled by Google's Firebase Authentication and never stored in the app's own database. Nobody on the project can read it.
Your consent record See The consent record below. It is how the project can show what you agreed to and when.

If you sign in with Google instead of a password, Google tells the app your name, email address, account identifier and a link to your profile picture. GuardBand never sees your Google password.

Your emergency contacts

You add at least one emergency contact, and the app stores each one's name, phone number and relationship to you. The band texts these people when an alert is raised, which is the entire reason they are collected.

You can also mark a contact as Emergency. That is stored with the contact and, at present, only changes how your list is sorted and labelled in the app.

These are other people's details, not yours. Please only add someone who knows they are your emergency contact and is willing to be texted. They did not sign up for GuardBand, and the project has no way to tell them their number is stored here — so that is your responsibility, not the app's.

From the band

Each alert or tracking update the band sends carries:

  • its location — latitude, longitude, and how accurate the reading is;
  • its battery level and whether it is charging;
  • the time of the reading;
  • the band's identifier and a sequence number.

This is the wearer's location. If the wearer is not you, they should know the band reports where they are — which is what the third part of the sign-up consent is about.

The phone's own location is never used. The app asks for no location permission at all. Everything on the map comes from the band.

No analytics or advertising

The app contains no analytics or advertising software. It does not collect an advertising identifier, and it does not track how you use it.

Who it is shared with

WhoWhat they get
Google (Firebase Authentication, Realtime Database) Everything above. Google stores and processes it on the project's behalf. The database is hosted in the asia-southeast1 region (Singapore).
MapTiler (map tiles) The map is served by a third-party tile provider, MapTiler. Each time the map loads, MapTiler receives your phone's IP address and the map tiles requested, which reveal the approximate area you are looking at. MapTiler does not receive your account details, your contacts or the band's reported position. The map data itself is from OpenStreetMap.
Google (profile picture) If you sign in with Google, the app shows your Google profile picture on the Profile tab and on the map, as the marker for the band's position. The picture is loaded from Google's image servers, which receive your phone's IP address when it is fetched. The app keeps a copy only in its own image cache on the phone; it is never stored in the project's database or uploaded anywhere. Accounts that sign in with an email and password show their initials instead, and nothing is fetched.
Google (place names) To show a place name such as a street and barangay on each alert instead of bare coordinates, the app asks your phone's built-in place-name service (Google's, on most Android phones) about each alert's position, rounded to about 11 metres, along with your phone's IP address. It is asked only while you are looking at alerts. The names are kept in the app's memory until it closes; they are not stored on the phone or in the project's database. If the service cannot be reached, the alert shows its coordinates instead.
Your emergency contacts An alert text. The band sends these, not the app, over its own mobile connection.

Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared for advertising.

Where it is stored, and on your phone

Your account and contacts live in Google's Firebase Realtime Database, in the asia-southeast1 region. Sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication.

On your phone itself, the app keeps very little:

  • your sign-in session, so you stay signed in;
  • a cache of map images, in the app's own temporary storage;
  • if you sign in with Google, a cached copy of your profile picture, in the same storage;
  • your display setting — light, dark, or the phone's own.

If you turn on Alert notifications in Settings, GuardBand keeps a connection open in the background to watch your band, and shows its alerts as notifications on this phone. They say only the type of alert, its time and the band's battery, never where you are; on a lock screen set to hide sensitive content they say only that there is a new alert. The app remembers only that the setting is on and which alert it showed last. Turn it off in Settings at any time.

Your contacts, your profile and the band's reports are not stored on the phone for offline use. Uninstalling the app removes the session, the caches and these settings; it does not delete your account.

One thing to be aware of while this is a student project. The band's reported locations are currently stored under a single shared band identifier, and the project's database rules for that part are open: that data can be read, and written, without signing in. There is no device pairing yet, so one person's band data is not separated from another's. Do not use GuardBand to protect anyone who would be put at risk by that. Your account details and your emergency contacts are not in that open area — they are readable only by you.

How long it is kept

Your account, profile, emergency contacts and consent record are kept until you delete your account. Nothing is deleted automatically, and there is no scheduled clean-up.

Alerts and tracking updates from the band are kept indefinitely at present, and deleting your account does not remove them — see the next section for why.

No retention period has been set for band reports yet. If one is, this notice will change to say what it is.

Your rights, and how to use them

See or correct your data

Your name, email and emergency contacts are all visible in the app, on the Profile and Contacts tabs, and the contacts can be edited there. For anything you cannot see or change yourself, write to julianmiguelroldan@gmail.com.

Delete your account

In the app: Track tab → the gear icon → Settings → Account information → Delete account. You will be asked to confirm who you are first.

That deletes, permanently:

  • your profile — name and email;
  • every emergency contact you saved, including their names and numbers;
  • your consent record;
  • your sign-in.

It does not delete the alerts and tracking updates the band recorded. Those sit under a band identifier that is shared between installations rather than tied to your account, so there is nothing there that can be matched to you and removed. The delete-account page explains this, and what to do if you cannot open the app.

One small record is also left behind: a marker, under your account's internal identifier, saying the account was deleted. It holds no name, no email and no contacts. It is what lets the app finish a deletion that was interrupted part way.

Withdraw your consent

Deleting your account is how you withdraw consent. There is no way to keep the account while taking the consent back, because the app needs the contacts and the band data to do the one thing it is for.

The consent record

When you finish signing up, the app records that you agreed, in the same write as your profile. An account that reaches the app without a record is shown the same agreement before it can go any further; declining signs you out and records nothing. The record stores:

  • that you accepted;
  • when, from the server's clock rather than your phone's;
  • the version of this notice you agreed to — 1.3 for the current wording.

It does not store your password, a reset code, or anything else. If this notice changes in a way that alters what is being agreed to, the version number goes up, and the project's change log records what changed.

If you accepted an earlier version, the app does not currently ask you to agree again. Your stored record keeps the version you actually accepted rather than being quietly updated.

Children

To make an account you must be 18 or older, or have a guardian's permission, and the sign-up asks you to confirm it. The app has no way to verify that.

The wearer of the band may well be a minor — that is a normal way to use a safety band — and the sign-up asks the person setting it up to confirm the wearer knows about and agrees to the tracking.

If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 18 who does not have a guardian's permission, we will delete it, in the same way as described on the delete-account page.

Changes to this notice

The version number and date at the top of this page change whenever the notice does. A change that alters what you are agreeing to also moves the version recorded with new consents, so the two can always be matched up.

Because this is a student project rather than a service with a mailing list, there is no notification when the notice changes. The dated version at the top of this page is the current one.
